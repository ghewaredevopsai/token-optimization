The same requests, as credits

A median request: ~0.6 credits. One of A's: ~17 credits, about 29 times as much. A's day: ~5,800 credits — 83% of a month's individual allowance, in a day.

Budget around the heaviest users, not the average. In a pooled plan the heaviest few spend the team's credits, and the average tells you nothing about whether that is happening.

The budget, annotated

What drops first, what never drops, what should not be there

Caching rewards you for not fiddling. A stable standing context bills at a tenth. Editing the instructions file every morning throws that discount away, which is a real cost of a prompt library nobody has put under review.

The tool-schema line is the hand-off to the MCP module, which owns that material and has a whole lab on it. One line here, no more, or the tiers start overlapping.

The audit

Four cuts, in this order, re-checking the answer after each

#CutTypically savesWhat it risks
1Drop the large data file nobody readsthe largest single slice nothing, until a question is actually about the data
2Replace a long document with the lines that decide itmost of the prose you have to know which lines — that is the skill
3Start a new chat instead of continuingthe whole conversation band re-establishing context you actually needed
4Stop attaching the docs folder at allthe rest this is the one that goes too far — find out where
Re-run the task after every cut, not at the end. An audit that only measures tokens proves you sent less, which was never in doubt. The column that matters is whether the answer is still right — and the cut that breaks it is the most valuable line in your record sheet.
Lab 3 now: 40% is the floor, and most rooms reach 70–85%. Finish by writing down what the meter could not see — that sentence is what makes your number defensible back at work.

Cut 4 is deliberately a step too far for most tasks. If nobody in the room breaks the answer, they have not cut hard enough and have learned less than the person who did. Say that before they start, and ask for a show of hands at the end for who broke it.
