Token Optimization

Two hours on what an AI-assisted request actually costs and what to do about it: where the tokens really go, choosing a model by use case rather than by reputation, cheap-first cascades, budgeting the context window, and the levers that live in your own code.

5 tiers · 5 labs · ~2 hours ~40% talk, ~60% hands-on Was token-economics until Sep 2026 Trainer: Rajesh Gheware

The tiers

The labs

All five run against Meridian Freight Desk. Two of them need no assistant at all, so they work on a locked-down machine and give every participant identical numbers. Start at hands-on/.

LabWhat you doWhat you write down
0 Count your own bundleMeter two bundles; check what your seat shows you Bundle sizes, how a conversation grows, credits visible or not
1 One matrix rowPredict first, in writing, then read 72 recorded runs How many of your three predictions survived the data
2 The cascadeWrite the gate, find the threshold, then deliberately break it Accuracy and cost at four thresholds, including the one that costs more than doing nothing
3 The 40% auditFour cuts in order, re-running the task after each Tokens and whether the answer is still right, per cut
4 Delete a model callReplace the counting half of a prompt with Python Tokens, wall time, and whether three runs agree with each other
Two instruments, and they never share a row. ctxmeter answers instantly and counts only what you listed; your billing page is authoritative about money and arrives late and aggregated. The counter proves the cut. The billing page proves the money.
Solutions ship with the module. solutions/ has a reference answer for every lab, including the exact threshold sweep and audit numbers.
Derive, do not assert. Every number on these slides traces to a measured run, and anything priced at a published rate is labelled a what-if. The estimator's own error is measured and published in the practice repo's tools/calibration.md, not claimed.

What this module covers, and what it does not

In scope. Where tokens actually go, the credit mechanism and plan defaults, model selection by use case, routing and cascades, context budgeting and eviction, and the levers in your own code — down to not calling the model at all.
Out of scope, deliberately. How to write a better prompt belongs to the Prompt & Context Engineering module. Running one task against three models is an existing lab in Copilot as Assistant and is analysed here, not repeated. What a tool list costs you per turn is one slide here and a whole tier in the MCP module. A cost-regression gate in CI belongs to the evaluation module when it is built.

The full list is in token-learning-outcomes.md, with where each outcome came from and which facts age fastest.
