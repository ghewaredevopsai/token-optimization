Tier 2

Routing and cascades

Cheap first, escalate on a rule. It is the highest-leverage pattern in this module and the easiest to get wrong — a bad cascade pays for both models and is slower than either.

4 slides · ~9 minutes Lab 2 follows · the cascade, and what it saved Trainer: Rajesh Gheware

Three topologies

One model, a router, or a cascade

The last line under the cascade is the whole risk. A router picks one path and pays once; a cascade pays the cheap call unconditionally and then sometimes pays again. That is fine at a low escalation rate and a disaster at a high one, and the lab lets people find the crossover themselves.

The gate

Escalate on a rule, not on a feeling

Gates that work

  • The cheap model's own confidence, below a threshold you measured
  • A validator rejected the shape
  • A test the answer must pass went red
  • The input is longer or more unusual than the cheap model handles

Gates that do not

  • "It looks unsure" — you cannot automate a feeling
  • Escalating every time, which costs more than never cascading
  • Escalating never, which is the cheap model with extra steps
  • A threshold somebody picked without running the numbers

The number to find

Your escalation rate, and the accuracy at that rate.

There is a threshold where accuracy reaches the strong model's and cost does not. Finding it is twenty lines of arithmetic, and it is Lab 2.

A cheap model that is confidently wrong cannot be cascaded — only replaced. The whole pattern rests on the cheap model knowing when it is guessing, and that is a property you have to check before you design around it.

The last warning is the one that kills real cascades. Confidence that does not correlate with correctness makes the gate a random sampler, and a random sampler that costs money is worse than either model alone. Have the room check the correlation before they tune a threshold.
